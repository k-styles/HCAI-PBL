Partial dependence

Force every penguin in the data to this value of {{ curve.pretty }}, ask the model, and average the answers.

Its weakness is built in: flipper length and body mass move together in real penguins, so setting a flipper to 230 mm while leaving body mass alone asks the model about an animal that does not exist. The curve is an average over impossible penguins as much as real ones.

Accumulated local effects

Split the range into bins and, inside each one, use only the penguins whose own {{ curve.pretty }} falls in that bin. Add the local changes up as you move along.

Nothing impossible is ever evaluated, which is the whole advantage. The price is that the curve shows change from the average rather than an absolute probability, so it is centred on zero and reads as “better or worse than typical”.
