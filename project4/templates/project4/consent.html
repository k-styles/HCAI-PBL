What this study is {% info "consent" %}

Comparing two ways of learning someone's film taste.

You will be shown films from a pool of well-known titles and asked which you would rather watch. Depending on which group you are placed in, that is either a series of choices between two films, or a series of lists of ten to put in order. After {{ minutes }} minutes you will answer {{ holdout }} further comparisons, and then be shown what your answers implied about your taste.

What is recorded

Which group you were placed in, which films you chose or how you ordered them, how long each task took, and your answer to the question below. Nothing that identifies you — no name, no email, no address, and no cookie beyond the one that keeps this page working.

What you can do

Stop at any point using the button on the study page; the run ends there and what you have done so far is fitted anyway. In the real study, withdrawing deletes your data and you are paid regardless.

Why the group is chosen at random

So that the difference between the two groups is the design and not the people in them. See {% term "between-subjects" "between subjects" %} for why you do not do both.

One question first

Used to balance the groups, not to screen you out.

How often do you watch a film? Someone who watches several a week has firmer opinions and will answer differently from someone who watches one a month, so the real study blocks the randomisation on this {% info "between-subjects" %}.
