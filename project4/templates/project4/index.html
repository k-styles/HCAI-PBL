The problem {% info "cold-start" %}

Why a recommender cannot start by watching you.

Every recommender you have used learned from millions of past ratings. The IMDB 5000 dataset contains none — no user has rated anything in it. That is not a gap in the dataset, it is the situation of every genuinely new user, and the only way out is to ask.

You will not answer two hundred questions, so the system gets perhaps twenty. The model is that each film is a vector x of {% term "feature-vector" "twenty features" %} and each person a vector w of {% term "preference-vector" "twenty weights" %}, with {% term "utility" "utility U(x) = wᵀx" %}. Elicitation is the business of recovering w from a handful of answers.

The two things being compared {% info "designs" %}

Neither is obviously better, which is what makes the study worth running.

Design 1 — pairwise choice

Two films, pick one, repeat. Each answer is small but it is quick and almost impossible to get wrong. Modelled by {% term "bradley-terry" "Bradley–Terry" %} directly.

Design 2 — rank ten

Ten films, dragged into order {% info "ranking" %}. One answer carries far more, but it is slow and the middle of a list of ten is genuinely hard. Needs the {% term "plackett-luce" "Plackett–Luce" %} extension.

A ranking of ten carries roughly sixty times the {% term "fisher" "information" %} of one pairwise comparison — which looks decisive until you fix the budget instead of the task count. Eight minutes buys 80 comparisons or 5 rankings, and the accumulated information then ties almost exactly.

What separates them is where that information points. Eighty comparisons touch 160 films; five rankings touch 50, and the 45 comparisons inside one ranking all live in the span of those ten. The result is a log-determinant 17.6 nats lower and a smallest eigenvalue six times smaller — directions of taste that ranking simply never constrains. A simulation of 120 participants per condition confirms it: pairwise ties when ranking is assumed flawless, and wins as soon as people get careless down the list.

This reverses the hypothesis the project started with, which is why both arms get the same {% term "confound" "eight-minute budget" %} rather than the same number of tasks, and why the direction was written down before any participant is recruited. The full argument is in the PDF.
